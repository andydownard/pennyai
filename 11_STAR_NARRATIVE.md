# 11-Star Narrative Brief

**Prepared for:** CEO Review
**Date:** March 2026
**Product:** Groflo (formerly Thriftly) / Penny AI

---

## The Narrative

Families waste hours every week on redundant grocery communication -- texting "did you get the milk?", buying duplicates, forgetting items someone else already handled. Penny eliminates that friction entirely. She becomes the household's shared memory so every family member trusts the list is accurate, claimed items are covered, and nothing falls through the cracks. The 11-Star Moment is the first time a family member checks the app, sees their partner already claimed the milk, and puts their phone away without sending a single text.

**One sentence:** "You never ask 'Did you get milk?' again."

---

## Features That PASS Gate 2

| # | Feature | Why It Passes |
|---|---------|---------------|
| 1 | **Household sharing (shared list)** | Every family member sees the same real-time list -- no one is out of the loop. |
| 2 | **Item claiming** | One tap to claim an item signals "I've got this," eliminating duplicate purchases and the need to coordinate via text. |
| 3 | **Penny restock nudges** | Penny proactively reminds the household when staples are running low, so no one has to remember or ask. |
| 4 | **Multi-channel input (SMS + email)** | Any family member can add items by texting or emailing Penny -- no app download required for the less tech-savvy member. |
| 5 | **Saved lists with auto-load** | Weekly grocery runs become one-tap -- the household's recurring needs are captured once and reloaded, so nothing gets forgotten. |

---

## Features That FAIL Gate 2 (Defer)

| # | Feature | Why It Fails |
|---|---------|--------------|
| 1 | **Dark mode** | Pure aesthetic preference. Does not reduce household uncertainty or improve coordination. Zero narrative alignment. |
| 2 | **Trip planner (store routing)** | Optimizes the solo shopping experience, not the household coordination problem. Convenience, not relief. |
| 3 | **Savings badges / gamification** | Motivational psychology, not coordination. A family that saves 12% still asks "did you get milk?" if they lack shared visibility. |

---

## Hero Messaging Options

### Option A: Direct Relief
- **Tagline:** "You never ask 'Did you get milk?' again."
- **Subhead:** Penny keeps your whole family on the same list -- so someone always has it covered.

### Option B: Shared Memory
- **Tagline:** "Your family's grocery brain."
- **Subhead:** Penny remembers what you need, knows who's grabbing it, and never lets anything slip through the cracks.

### Option C: Trust the List
- **Tagline:** "Text Penny. She handles the rest."
- **Subhead:** One shared list. Real-time claims. No more duplicate trips, forgotten items, or "did you get my text?"

---

## KPIs That Measure the Narrative

| # | KPI | Target | Why It Matters |
|---|-----|--------|----------------|
| 1 | **Household activation rate** | 40% of users invite a household member within 14 days | The narrative only works with 2+ people on a shared list. Single-user households cannot experience the 11-Star Moment. |
| 2 | **Item claim rate** | 30% of items in multi-member households are claimed before checkout | Claiming is the behavioral proof that coordination is happening inside the app instead of via text/call. |
| 3 | **Multi-member weekly active rate** | 60% of households have 2+ members active in same week | If only one person uses the app, the family reverts to "did you get milk?" -- both members must stay engaged for the narrative to hold. |
